# A Brief History of the Internet

The internet as we know it today evolved over several decades from a small government-funded research network into a global system connecting billions of devices.

## ARPANET: The Origins

The foundation of the modern internet traces back to ARPANET, a project funded by the U.S. Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA). The first message was sent over ARPANET on October 29, 1969, between computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute. The system was designed to allow research institutions to share computing resources and to survive partial network failures.

## The Birth of TCP/IP

In the 1970s, researchers Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the Transmission Control Protocol and Internet Protocol (TCP/IP), a set of rules that allowed different computer networks to communicate with each other reliably. TCP/IP was formally adopted as the standard for ARPANET on January 1, 1983, an event sometimes referred to as the "flag day" that effectively marks the birth of the modern internet.

## Domain Names and Email

The Domain Name System (DNS), which translates human-readable addresses like "example.com" into numerical IP addresses, was introduced in 1983 by Paul Mockapetris. Email predates the modern internet's public rollout; the first networked email was sent in 1971 by Ray Tomlinson, who is also credited with popularizing the "@" symbol to separate usernames from host names.

## The World Wide Web

The World Wide Web, often confused with the internet itself, was invented by British computer scientist Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 while working at CERN. He created the first web browser, the first web server, and the HTML markup language. The web was made publicly available in 1991, and it introduced the concept of hyperlinks that let users navigate between documents.

## Commercialization and Growth

Through the early 1990s, the internet was largely restricted to academic and government use. The National Science Foundation lifted restrictions on commercial use of the internet backbone in 1995, which opened the door to rapid commercial growth. The number of internet users grew from an estimated 16 million people in 1995 to over 5 billion people by the early 2020s.

## Mobile and Broadband Era

The 2000s and 2010s saw the shift from dial-up to broadband connections, and later the rise of mobile internet access through smartphones following the 2007 launch of the iPhone. By the early 2020s, mobile devices accounted for more than half of global internet traffic, fundamentally changing how people access online content.
