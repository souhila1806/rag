# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy, usually from the sun, into chemical energy stored in glucose. It is the foundation of nearly all food chains on Earth and is responsible for producing the oxygen that most living organisms depend on.

## The Basic Equation

The overall chemical reaction of photosynthesis can be summarized as: six molecules of carbon dioxide plus six molecules of water, using light energy, produce one molecule of glucose and six molecules of oxygen. This reaction takes place primarily in the leaves of plants, within specialized organelles called chloroplasts.

## Chloroplasts and Chlorophyll

Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, which absorbs light most efficiently in the blue and red wavelengths while reflecting green light, which is why most plants appear green. Each chloroplast contains stacks of membrane structures called thylakoids, organized into groups known as grana, where the light-dependent reactions occur.

## Light-Dependent Reactions

In the light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses that energy to split water molecules, a process called photolysis. This releases oxygen as a byproduct and generates energy-carrying molecules called ATP and NADPH, which are used in the next stage. These reactions occur within the thylakoid membranes.

## The Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle, also called the light-independent reactions, takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast. It uses the ATP and NADPH produced earlier to convert carbon dioxide from the air into glucose through a series of enzyme-driven steps. Unlike the light-dependent reactions, the Calvin cycle does not require direct light, though it depends on the products of those earlier reactions.

## Factors Affecting Photosynthesis Rate

The rate of photosynthesis is influenced by light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. Increasing light intensity generally increases the rate of photosynthesis up to a saturation point, beyond which other factors become limiting. Most plants photosynthesize most efficiently between 25°C and 35°C; temperatures much higher than this can damage the enzymes involved.

## Importance to Life on Earth

Photosynthesis produces an estimated 100 to 115 billion metric tons of organic carbon per year across terrestrial and ocean ecosystems combined. It is also the primary source of atmospheric oxygen, with ocean phytoplankton alone estimated to contribute roughly half of the world's oxygen production.
