# The Seven Wonders of the Ancient World

The Seven Wonders of the Ancient World is a list of remarkable constructions of classical antiquity, first compiled by Greek writers such as Antipater of Sidon around the 2nd century BCE. Of the seven, only one survives largely intact today.

## Great Pyramid of Giza

Built around 2560 BCE in Egypt as a tomb for Pharaoh Khufu, the Great Pyramid is the oldest of the Seven Wonders and the only one still standing. It originally stood about 146.6 meters tall and was covered in polished limestone casing stones, most of which have since eroded or been removed. It held the record as the tallest man-made structure in the world for over 3,800 years.

## Hanging Gardens of Babylon

According to ancient accounts, the Hanging Gardens were built in Babylon, in modern-day Iraq, possibly around the 6th century BCE under King Nebuchadnezzar II. They were described as a series of tiered gardens with a wide variety of trees, shrubs, and vines. No archaeological evidence has been definitively confirmed, and some historians debate whether the gardens existed in Babylon at all or were actually located in Nineveh.

## Statue of Zeus at Olympia

Sculpted by the Greek artist Phidias around 435 BCE, this massive seated statue of Zeus stood inside the Temple of Zeus in Olympia, Greece. It was made of ivory and gold plates over a wooden frame and stood roughly 12 meters tall. The statue was eventually destroyed, likely by fire, sometime in the 5th century CE.

## Temple of Artemis at Ephesus

Located in present-day Turkey, the Temple of Artemis was rebuilt several times after destruction by flood and arson. Its most famous version, funded by King Croesus of Lydia, was completed around 550 BCE and featured 127 marble columns roughly 18 meters high. It was destroyed by the Goths in 268 CE.

## Mausoleum at Halicarnassus

Constructed between 353 and 350 BCE as a tomb for Mausolus, a satrap of the Persian Empire, this structure in modern-day Bodrum, Turkey, gave rise to the word "mausoleum." It combined Greek, Egyptian, and Lycian architectural styles and stood around 45 meters tall. It was destroyed by a series of earthquakes between the 12th and 15th centuries CE.

## Colossus of Rhodes

A giant bronze statue of the sun god Helios, erected in the city of Rhodes, Greece, around 280 BCE to celebrate a military victory. It stood approximately 33 meters tall, comparable in height to the Statue of Liberty. The statue collapsed during an earthquake in 226 BCE, only about 54 years after it was completed.

## Lighthouse of Alexandria

Built on the island of Pharos near Alexandria, Egypt, around 280 BCE during the reign of Ptolemy II, this lighthouse guided sailors into the harbor for centuries. Estimates of its height range from 100 to 140 meters, making it one of the tallest structures of the ancient world. It was damaged by earthquakes between the 10th and 14th centuries CE and eventually collapsed entirely by 1480 CE.
